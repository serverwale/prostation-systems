Multi-Monitor Workstation Checklist for Trading Desks and Control Rooms

How many screens a computer can run is decided by its graphics outputs and display bandwidth, not by processor speed. For a trading desk or control room the machine must also stay up all day. This is a short checklist; the full reasoning is in ProStation's guide to a multi-monitor workstation for trading desks and control rooms.

Physical screens must not exceed the outputs on the cards you install. For five or more screens, plan a second card instead of stretching one with adapters.

Check cable bandwidth

  • DisplayPort 1.4: 32.4 Gbps total, about 25.92 Gbps usable — comfortable for one 4K 60 Hz screen per port.
  • HDMI 2.0: 18 Gbps total; a 4K 60 Hz signal uses roughly 14.4 Gbps.
  • DisplayPort daisy-chaining (MST): the chain shares one link, so it suits lower-resolution screens. For critical desks, use one cable per monitor so no screen depends on another.

Video walls

NVIDIA Mosaic spans one desktop across up to 16 panels from a single system and is listed for NVIDIA's professional graphics products. Most trading desks do not need it; NOC, SOC and plant control rooms might. Confirm the exact card against NVIDIA's supported-product information.

FAQ

  • How many monitors can one graphics card run? — It depends on the card. The RTX A4000 lists four DisplayPort outputs and the GeForce RTX 4090 lists three DisplayPort and one HDMI with up to four displays. For more than four screens you normally add a second card.
  • Is DisplayPort better than HDMI for multiple monitors? — DisplayPort 1.4 carries about 25.92 Gbps of data versus 18 Gbps total for HDMI 2.0, so it has more headroom. Use whichever input your monitors support, with one direct cable per screen.
  • Can I daisy-chain monitors on a trading desk? — Yes, if each monitor supports DisplayPort 1.2 or newer, but the chain shares one link. For critical desks, use one cable per monitor.
