Server for a Real Estate Agency: CRM, Portal and Media Checklist

A real estate agency's server runs a CRM, a listing site and a large archive of photos, brochures and agreements. That is light on processor power and heavy on storage, backups and uptime. This is a short checklist; the full guide is ProStation's server guide for real estate agencies.

What the software asks for

CRM vendors publish software requirements rather than hardware minimums. EspoCRM's documentation, for example, lists PHP 8.3 to 8.5, MySQL 8.0+, MariaDB 10.3+ or PostgreSQL 15, a PHP memory_limit of 256M and a cron setup for background jobs. Size by users, records and attachments, and check the requirements of the exact release you will run.

Indian rules that touch the data

General information, not legal advice. Registered real estate agents must keep books of account and records under RERA Sections 9 and 10 and the state rules. A CRM also holds personal data, and under India's DPDP Act the Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with core obligations such as security safeguards and breach notification taking effect on 13 May 2027, according to Hogan Lovells' summary. The Rules refer to safeguards such as encryption, access controls, monitoring and backups, and to keeping logs for at least one year. Owning the server is a way to control the data, not a legal requirement; take advice for your organisation. See also secure document server design.

FAQ

  • What server does a real estate CRM need? — It depends on users, records and attachments. EspoCRM lists PHP 8.3 to 8.5 and MySQL, MariaDB or PostgreSQL. A small agency typically needs ECC memory, NVMe for the database, extra disks for media and a tested backup.
  • Should an agency host its CRM on its own server or in the cloud? — Either can work. A subscription CRM suits small teams without IT staff; an owned server suits many users, large media archives or a self-hosted CRM. Many agencies host the public listing site in the cloud and keep the CRM and documents on their own server.
  • Does DPDP law require data to stay on my own server? — Not as a general rule. The DPDP Act requires reasonable security safeguards and breach reporting wherever data is hosted; the Rules' core obligations take effect on 13 May 2027. Take legal advice.
  • Is RAID enough to protect property records? — No. RAID keeps a server running through a disk failure but does not protect against deletion, ransomware or fire. Add a tested backup with an off-site copy.
