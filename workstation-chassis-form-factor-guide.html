Mini-ITX vs Micro-ATX vs Full Tower: Choosing a Workstation Chassis

Chassis choice gets picked last and thought about least, but it quietly caps almost everything else in a custom build — how many GPUs actually fit, how much airflow the cooling guide's advice can even work with, and whether the build can be upgraded in two years or has to be replaced outright.

Mini-ITX — smallest footprint, real tradeoffs

Fits on a desk without dominating it, and modern Mini-ITX boards support surprisingly capable single-GPU builds. But there's usually room for exactly one GPU, one or two storage drives, and a single tower cooler or small AIO — no path to add a second card or extra drives later without a full rebuild.

Micro-ATX — the practical middle ground

Enough board space for a full-size GPU plus a modest second expansion card, more storage bays, and better airflow paths than most Mini-ITX cases without stepping up to full-tower dimensions. This is where most single-GPU AI/render/virtualization builds that still want future headroom actually land.

Full tower — built for multi-GPU and dense storage

  • Multi-GPU AI training or render nodes — the only realistic option once you're running 2–3 GPUs; spacing and airflow between cards matters as much as raw slot count.
  • NAS/storage-heavy builds — drive-bay count scales with case size; a storage server with 8+ bays simply doesn't exist in a compact form factor.
  • Serious cooling — room for large air coolers or multiple radiators, which matters directly for the thermal headroom discussed in our cooling & noise guide.

The real question isn't size, it's the build 12 months from now

A compact case is the right call for a fixed, single-purpose build that will never be touched again. Anything expected to grow — a second GPU added later, more storage, a cooling upgrade — is worth sizing up front, because retrofitting a bigger case after the fact means a second build, not an upgrade.
